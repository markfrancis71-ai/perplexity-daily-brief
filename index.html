On This Day · April 27

711
Moorish Invasion of Iberia Begins

Moorish troops led by Tariq ibn Ziyad landed at Gibraltar on this date, launching the Islamic conquest of the Iberian Peninsula. The campaign rapidly swept across Visigothic Spain, establishing Al-Andalus and reshaping European civilization for nearly eight centuries.

1521
Ferdinand Magellan Killed in the Philippines

Portuguese explorer Ferdinand Magellan was killed at the Battle of Mactan in the Philippines, struck down by warriors led by Chief Lapu-Lapu after he intervened in a local dispute. Though Magellan did not survive, his expedition continued under Juan Sebastián Elcano and completed the first circumnavigation of the globe.

1667
Milton Sells Paradise Lost for £10

Blind and poverty-stricken, the poet John Milton sold the manuscript of Paradise Lost to a printer for just £10 so it could be entered into the Stationers' Register and published. The epic poem went on to become one of the greatest works in the English language, reshaping conceptions of creation, free will, and the Fall.

1810
Beethoven Composes "Für Elise"

Ludwig van Beethoven wrote the Bagatelle in A minor, famously known as "Für Elise," a short piano piece that would become one of his most recognized and beloved compositions. The piece remained unpublished during his lifetime and was only discovered and released 40 years after his death.

1865
Sultana Steamboat Disaster

The steamboat Sultana, overloaded with recently freed Union prisoners of war, suffered a catastrophic boiler explosion on the Mississippi River near Memphis, Tennessee. Estimated death tolls range from 1,500 to over 1,800, making it the deadliest maritime disaster in U.S. history — largely overshadowed at the time by the recent assassination of President Lincoln.

1961
Sierra Leone Gains Independence

The United Kingdom granted Sierra Leone its independence on this date, making it a sovereign nation and a member of the Commonwealth. The country, established in 1787 as a settlement for freed slaves and Black loyalists, had been a British protectorate for over a century before achieving self-rule.

1967
Expo 67 Opens in Montreal

The World's Fair known as Expo 67 officially opened in Montreal, Canada, with a grand ceremony broadcast around the world. Themed "Man and His World," the exposition attracted over 50 million visitors and is widely considered one of the most successful World's Fairs of the 20th century, showcasing futuristic architecture including Buckminster Fuller's iconic geodesic dome.

1981
Xerox PARC Introduces the Computer Mouse to the World

Xerox PARC publicly demonstrated the computer mouse as part of its Alto workstation system, a pivotal moment in human-computer interaction. Though invented earlier by Douglas Engelbart, the Xerox demonstration brought the device into the commercial spotlight and directly inspired the graphical interfaces later adopted by Apple and Microsoft.

1994
South Africa's First All-Race Democratic Election

South Africa held its first fully democratic general election, allowing Black citizens to vote for the first time in the country's history, effectively ending the apartheid era. Nelson Mandela and the African National Congress won decisively, and Mandela was inaugurated as the country's first Black president on May 10, 1994.

2005
Airbus A380 Makes Maiden Flight

The Airbus A380, the world's largest commercial passenger aircraft, completed its maiden test flight from Toulouse, France, marking a landmark achievement in aviation engineering. The double-deck, wide-body jet was designed to carry up to 853 passengers in an all-economy configuration and represented a $25 billion development program by Airbus.
