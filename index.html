On This Day · April 20

1303
Sapienza University of Rome Founded

Pope Boniface VIII issued a papal bull establishing the Sapienza University of Rome, making it one of the oldest universities in the world. It would grow to become one of Europe's largest academic institutions.

1653
Cromwell Dissolves the Rump Parliament

Oliver Cromwell forcibly dissolved England's Rump Parliament, ending the Long Parliament that had sat since 1640. This act paved the way for the Protectorate and Cromwell's personal rule over the Commonwealth.

1770
Captain Cook Arrives in Australia

British explorer Captain James Cook arrived at Botany Bay in New South Wales, becoming one of the first Europeans to make contact with the eastern coast of Australia. His voyage would have profound consequences for the future colonization of the continent.

1792
France Declares War, Starting the French Revolutionary Wars

Revolutionary France declared war against Austria and Prussia, launching the French Revolutionary Wars that would engulf much of Europe for over two decades. The conflict ultimately gave rise to Napoleon Bonaparte's ascent to power.

1841
Poe Publishes First Modern Detective Story

Edgar Allan Poe's 'The Murders in the Rue Morgue' was published in Graham's Magazine, introducing detective C. Auguste Dupin and pioneering the detective fiction genre. The story is widely regarded as the first modern detective story in literary history.

1862
Pasteur and Bernard Complete Pasteurization Experiment

Louis Pasteur and Claude Bernard completed the first successful pasteurization test, disproving the long-held theory of spontaneous generation. The experiment demonstrated that microorganisms in liquids could be killed by heat, laying the groundwork for modern food safety and germ theory.

1902
Curies Isolate Radium Chloride

Pierre and Marie Curie successfully isolated radium chloride, confirming the existence of the radioactive element radium. The achievement earned the couple the Nobel Prize in Physics the following year and revolutionized the understanding of atomic science.

1912
Fenway Park and Tiger Stadium Open

Both Fenway Park in Boston and Navin Field (later Tiger Stadium) in Detroit opened on the same day, each hosting their first professional baseball games. Fenway Park remains the oldest active ballpark in Major League Baseball.

1972
Apollo 16 Lands on the Moon

NASA's Apollo 16 Lunar Module Orion, commanded by John Young with Charles Duke as pilot, touched down on the Moon's Descartes Highlands. The mission was the fifth crewed lunar landing and the first to explore a highlands region of the Moon.

2010
Deepwater Horizon Explosion

The Deepwater Horizon oil drilling rig exploded in the Gulf of Mexico, killing eleven workers and triggering the largest accidental marine oil spill in history. The BP-operated well gushed for 87 days, releasing an estimated 4.9 million barrels of oil before it was finally capped.
