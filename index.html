On This Day · April 24

1558
Mary, Queen of Scots Weds the French Dauphin

Mary Stuart, Queen of Scots, married François, the Dauphin of France, at Notre-Dame de Paris in a lavish royal ceremony. The union briefly made Mary heir to the French throne, cementing a powerful political alliance between Scotland and France known as the Auld Alliance.

1704
First Regular American Newspaper Published

The Boston News-Letter became the first continuously published newspaper in British Colonial America, issued by postmaster John Campbell. It marked a turning point in colonial public life, establishing the precedent for a free press in what would become the United States.

1792
"La Marseillaise" Composed

French army officer Claude-Joseph Rouget de Lisle composed "Chant de guerre pour l'Armée du Rhin" in a single night in Strasbourg, originally written to inspire French troops. The stirring anthem was adopted by revolutionary Marseille battalions marching on Paris, earning the name "La Marseillaise," and became France's national anthem in 1795.

1800
Library of Congress Founded

President John Adams signed legislation appropriating $5,000 to purchase books "necessary for the use of Congress," officially establishing the Library of Congress. It has since grown into the largest library in the world, holding more than 170 million items across its collections.

1916
Easter Rising in Dublin

Approximately 1,600 Irish republicans, led by Patrick Pearse and James Connolly, seized key sites across Dublin and proclaimed an Irish Republic, launching an armed rebellion against British rule. The uprising was suppressed after six days of fierce fighting, but the execution of its leaders galvanized Irish public opinion and ultimately paved the way for Irish independence.

1953
Winston Churchill Knighted

Queen Elizabeth II knighted former Prime Minister Winston Churchill at Buckingham Palace, bestowing upon him the Order of the Garter, Britain's highest order of chivalry. Churchill, who had led Britain through World War II and was serving his second term as Prime Minister at the time, became Sir Winston Churchill.

1967
First Human Spaceflight Fatality: Vladimir Komarov

Soviet cosmonaut Vladimir Komarov was killed when his Soyuz 1 spacecraft's parachute system failed to deploy properly during re-entry, causing the capsule to crash into the earth at high speed. Komarov became the first person to die during a space mission, and the tragedy led to a nearly two-year halt in Soviet crewed spaceflight while the Soyuz craft was redesigned.

1970
China Launches Its First Satellite

The People's Republic of China launched Dong Fang Hong I ("The East Is Red") aboard a Long March 1 rocket, becoming the fifth nation to independently place a satellite into Earth's orbit. The satellite continuously broadcast the patriotic song "The East Is Red," symbolizing China's arrival as a space power.

1981
IBM Personal Computer Introduced

IBM officially introduced its first Personal Computer (IBM 5150), marking a watershed moment in the history of computing and the beginning of the modern PC era. The machine used an open architecture and Intel 8088 processor, setting standards that shaped the entire personal computing industry for decades.

1990
Hubble Space Telescope Launched
