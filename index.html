On This Day · April 28

1503
Battle of Cerignola — First Battle Won by Gunpowder Small Arms

Spanish forces under Gonzalo de Córdoba defeated the French army at Cerignola in southern Italy, making it one of the earliest European battles decided by firearms rather than pikes or swords. The Spanish used arquebuses with devastating effect from behind field fortifications, killing French commander Louis d'Armagnac, Duke of Nemours. The victory marked a pivotal shift in European military tactics toward gunpowder-based warfare.

1686
Newton's Principia Mathematica Published

The first volume of Isaac Newton's landmark work Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica was published, laying out his laws of motion and universal gravitation. The work unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics under a single mathematical framework, fundamentally transforming physics and astronomy. It is widely regarded as one of the most influential scientific works ever written.

1789
Mutiny on the HMS Bounty

Fletcher Christian led a mutiny against Lieutenant William Bligh aboard the British Royal Navy vessel HMS Bounty in the South Pacific, three weeks after departing Tahiti. Bligh and 18 loyal crew members were set adrift in a 23-foot open boat, while the mutineers eventually settled on remote Pitcairn Island. Bligh remarkably navigated nearly 4,000 miles to safety, and the incident became one of history's most famous maritime mutinies.

1788
Maryland Ratifies the U.S. Constitution

Maryland became the seventh state to ratify the United States Constitution, bringing the new federal framework closer to the nine-state threshold needed for ratification. The vote marked an important milestone in the formation of the United States government following independence from Britain. Maryland's ratification helped build momentum that led to full ratification by New Hampshire just weeks later.

1869
Transcontinental Railroad: Ten Miles of Track in One Day

Chinese and Irish laborers working for the Central Pacific Railroad laid ten miles of track in a single day across Nevada, setting a record that has never been surpassed. The feat was accomplished by a crew of around 4,000 workers using hand tools and was executed as a deliberate challenge to the competing Union Pacific Railroad. The transcontinental railroad was completed just over a week later at Promontory Summit, Utah.

1937
Saddam Hussein Born

Saddam Hussein was born in the village of Al-Awjah near Tikrit, Iraq, into a poor peasant family. He rose through the Ba'ath Party to become President of Iraq in 1979, ruling for nearly 24 years through a combination of political repression and cult of personality. He was captured by U.S. forces in 2003 following the invasion of Iraq and executed in Baghdad in December 2006.

1945
Benito Mussolini Executed

Italian dictator Benito Mussolini and his mistress Clara Petacci were shot dead by Italian partisan Walter Audisio near Lake Como as they attempted to flee to Switzerland in the final days of World War II in Europe. Their bodies were subsequently transported to Milan and hung upside down in the Piazzale Loreto, the same square where partisans had been executed by fascists the previous year. Mussolini's death effectively ended the Italian Social Republic and two decades of fascist rule in Italy.

1947
Kon-Tiki Expedition Sets Sail

Norwegian explorer Thor Heyerdahl and five crew members departed Callao, Peru, aboard the balsa-wood raft Kon-Tiki to test his theory that ancient Peruvians could have sailed to Polynesia. The raft had no modern navigation aids and was built using only materials available to pre-Columbian peoples. After 101 days at sea the expedition successfully crossed approximately 4,300 miles of the Pacific Ocean, arriving at the Tuamotu Islands in Polynesia.

1967
Muhammad Ali Refuses Military Induction

Heavyweight boxing champion Muhammad Ali refused induction into the U.S. Army in Houston, Texas, citing his Islamic faith and opposition to the Vietnam War with the famous declaration, 'I ain't got no quarrel with them Viet Cong.' He was immediately stripped of his world title and boxing license, and was later convicted of draft evasion — a conviction the U.S. Supreme Court unanimously overturned in 1971. Ali's stand became a defining moment of civil rights and anti-war activism in the 1960s.

2001
First Space Tourist Launches to the ISS

American businessman Dennis Tito launched aboard a Russian Soyuz rocket from Baikonur Cosmodrome, becoming the world's first paying space tourist. Tito paid approximately $20 million for a six-day stay aboard the International Space Station alongside two Russian cosmonauts, over the objections of NASA. His flight opened the door to commercial human spaceflight and inspired subsequent private space tourism ventures.
