1429
Joan of Arc Lifts the Siege of Orléans

On this day, the teenage French commander Joan of Arc arrived in the besieged city of Orléans during the Hundred Years' War, inspiring its demoralized defenders. Her arrival galvanized French forces, whose counterattacks compelled the English to abandon the siege within days. The relief of Orléans is widely regarded as the turning point of the war and cemented Joan of Arc's legendary status.

1770
James Cook Lands at Botany Bay, Australia

British explorer Captain James Cook made his first landing on the Australian continent at a harbor he named Botany Bay, near present-day Sydney. Cook and his crew aboard HMS Endeavour were the first Europeans to chart the eastern coastline of Australia. The landing marked the beginning of British awareness of the continent and ultimately led to its colonization.

1862
Union Forces Capture New Orleans

Union Admiral David Farragut's fleet forced its way past Confederate river defenses and captured New Orleans, the Confederacy's largest city and most vital port. Farragut entered the city with 250 Marines and replaced the Louisiana state flag with the Union flag over city hall. The loss of New Orleans and control of the mouth of the Mississippi River dealt a severe strategic blow to the Confederate cause.

1913
Gideon Sundback Patents the Modern Zipper

Swedish-American engineer Gideon Sundback was granted a U.S. patent for an improved 'separable fastener' — the device that became the modern zipper. His design featured interlocking teeth on two tapes drawn together by a sliding clasp, a significant improvement over earlier hook-and-eye predecessors. The invention went on to transform clothing, luggage, and countless other industries worldwide.

1945
Dachau Concentration Camp Liberated

American troops from the U.S. Seventh Army liberated Dachau, the first and one of the most notorious Nazi concentration camps, located outside Munich, Germany. Soldiers discovered tens of thousands of survivors in desperate condition, along with rail cars filled with the corpses of prisoners killed during a forced death march. The liberation of Dachau became a defining symbol of the Holocaust's horrors and the Allied victory in Europe.

1967
Muhammad Ali Stripped of Boxing Title

One day after refusing induction into the U.S. Army on grounds of religious conscience, heavyweight champion Muhammad Ali was stripped of his boxing title by the World Boxing Association. Ali, a member of the Nation of Islam, famously declared he had 'no quarrel with them Viet Cong.' He was subsequently convicted of draft evasion — a conviction later overturned by the Supreme Court — and did not fight professionally for more than three years.

1975
Operation Frequent Wind: U.S. Evacuates Saigon

As North Vietnamese forces closed in on the South Vietnamese capital, the United States launched Operation Frequent Wind, the largest helicopter evacuation in history. Over 24 hours, U.S. helicopters airlifted approximately 7,000 American civilians and at-risk Vietnamese nationals from Saigon. Saigon fell to North Vietnamese forces the following day, marking the end of U.S. involvement in the Vietnam War.

1992
Los Angeles Riots Begin After Rodney King Verdict

After a jury acquitted four Los Angeles Police Department officers of charges stemming from the videotaped beating of Black motorist Rodney King, six days of widespread rioting erupted across Los Angeles. The unrest resulted in 63 deaths, more than 2,300 injuries, and over $1 billion in property damage before 10,000 National Guard troops restored order. The riots ignited a national reckoning on racial inequity and police use of force that continues to shape American public discourse.

1997
Chemical Weapons Convention Enters into Force

The Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC), negotiated in 1993, officially entered into force on this date, becoming one of the most comprehensive disarmament treaties in history. The treaty banned the development, production, stockpiling, and use of chemical weapons among its signatory nations. It established the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) to monitor compliance, which went on to win the Nobel Peace Prize in 2013.

2011
Royal Wedding of Prince William and Catherine Middleton

Prince William of Wales married Catherine Middleton in a lavish ceremony at Westminster Abbey in London, watched by an estimated 2 billion television viewers worldwide. The couple had met as students at the University of St Andrews in Scotland and became engaged in 2010. The wedding was a global cultural event, with celebrations taking place across the United Kingdom and beyond, and the couple were granted the titles Duke and Duchess of Cambridge.
