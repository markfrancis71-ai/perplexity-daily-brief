On This Day · April 23

1564
Shakespeare Born in Stratford-upon-Avon

William Shakespeare, widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language, is traditionally celebrated as having been born on April 23 in Stratford-upon-Avon, England — a date chosen to align with his baptismal record of April 26 and with St. George's Day. He went on to author at least 37 plays and 154 sonnets, transforming the English language and theatrical tradition. By remarkable coincidence, he also died on the same date in 1616, aged 52.

1616
Shakespeare Dies on St. George's Day

William Shakespeare died on April 23, 1616 — St. George's Day — the same date on which he is traditionally said to have been born 52 years earlier. His death marked the end of the most celebrated literary career in the English language, though his works were posthumously collected and published in the First Folio of 1623. The symmetry of his birth and death on England's patron saint's feast day has made April 23 a globally recognized day of literature, now also observed as UNESCO's World Book Day.

1661
Charles II Crowned King of England

Charles II was formally crowned at Westminster Abbey on April 23, 1661, restoring the English monarchy after more than a decade of Puritan rule under Oliver Cromwell's Commonwealth. The lavish coronation was the first to be fully documented and helped establish the ceremonial traditions still used in British coronations today. Charles's reign ushered in the Restoration period, marked by a flourishing of theatre, science (including the founding of the Royal Society), and a relaxation of Puritan social restrictions.

1789
President Washington Takes Residence in New York

Newly inaugurated President George Washington moved into Franklin House (also known as the Samuel Osgood House) in New York City on April 23, 1789, establishing it as the first presidential residence in the United States. New York was then the nation's capital, and Washington's arrival marked the practical beginning of the executive branch of the new republic. He would reside there until the capital moved to Philadelphia in 1790.

1896
First Commercial Film Screening in New York City

On April 23, 1896, Thomas Edison's Vitascope projector gave its first commercial public demonstration at Koster and Bial's Music Hall in New York City, marking a landmark moment in the birth of cinema. Audiences watched a series of short film clips, including scenes of ocean waves and dancing, projected onto a large screen — a technology that would transform global entertainment. The event is considered one of the earliest instances of projected motion pictures shown to a paying audience in America.

1920
Turkish Grand National Assembly Holds First Meeting

The Turkish Grand National Assembly convened for the first time on April 23, 1920, in Ankara, under the leadership of Mustafa Kemal (later Atatürk), forming the basis of the modern Turkish state. This assembly rejected the authority of the Ottoman Sultan and laid the groundwork for the Turkish War of Independence and the eventual proclamation of the Republic of Turkey in 1923. April 23 is now celebrated as National Sovereignty and Children's Day in Turkey, a public holiday honoring the founding of the parliament.

1954
Hank Aaron Hits His First Major League Home Run

On April 23, 1954, Hank Aaron of the Milwaukee Braves hit the first of what would become 755 career home runs, a record that stood for over three decades. Aaron went on to become one of baseball's all-time greats, breaking Babe Ruth's long-standing record of 714 home runs in 1974. His perseverance in the face of racial hostility during his pursuit of the record made his achievement one of the most significant in American sports history.

1985
Coca-Cola Launches "New Coke"

On April 23, 1985, Coca-Cola CEO Roberto Goizueta unveiled a reformulated sweeter version of Coke — dubbed "New Coke" — at a press conference in New York City, discontinuing the 99-year-old original formula. The backlash was swift and intense, with consumers calling it one of the worst marketing blunders in corporate history; Coca-Cola received thousands of angry calls per day and protests were held across the country. Just 79 days later, the company reversed course and reintroduced the original as "Coca-Cola Classic," in one of history's most memorable lessons in brand loyalty.

1993
César Chávez Dies

Labor leader and civil rights activist César Chávez died on April 23, 1993, in San Luis, Arizona, at the age of 66. Chávez co-founded the United Farm Workers union and spent decades organizing migrant agricultural laborers through nonviolent tactics including boycotts, strikes, and hunger strikes, dramatically improving working conditions for some of America's most vulnerable workers. He has since become an enduring symbol of the American labor movement, and April 23 is observed as César Chávez Day in several U.S. states.

2005
First Video Uploaded to YouTube

On April 23, 2005, YouTube co-founder Jawed Karim uploaded the platform's first-ever video, a 19-second clip titled "Me at the zoo," filmed at the San Diego Zoo. The upload marked the quiet beginning of what would become the world's dominant video-sharing platform, acquired by Google just 18 months later for $1.65 billion. The video has since surpassed 300 million views and stands as a milestone in the history of the internet and digital media.
