On This Day · April 22

1500
Pedro Álvares Cabral Discovers Brazil

Portuguese navigator Pedro Álvares Cabral sighted the mainland of South America near present-day Porto Seguro, Brazil, while on a voyage tracing Vasco da Gama's route to India. He claimed the land for Portugal, making this the first European contact with Brazil and one of the most consequential moments in the history of the Americas.

1724
Birth of Immanuel Kant

German philosopher Immanuel Kant was born in Königsberg, Prussia (now Kaliningrad, Russia). His landmark works, including the Critique of Pure Reason, fundamentally reshaped Western philosophy and established frameworks in epistemology, ethics, and metaphysics that remain deeply influential today.

1864
"In God We Trust" Authorized on U.S. Currency

The U.S. Congress passed the Coinage Act of 1864, permitting the inscription "In God We Trust" to be placed on all coins minted as United States currency. The phrase first appeared on the two-cent coin that year and was eventually extended to all U.S. paper currency by 1957.

1876
First National League Baseball Game

The first official National League baseball game was played at the Jefferson Street Grounds in Philadelphia, marking the formal start of what would become the oldest surviving professional sports league in America. The Philadelphia Athletics defeated the Boston Red Stockings, and the NL went on to shape the structure of Major League Baseball for over a century.

1889
Oklahoma Land Rush Begins

At the stroke of noon, a single gunshot signaled the start of the Oklahoma Land Rush, as tens of thousands of settlers raced to claim nearly two million acres of unassigned federal land. Within hours, the cities of Oklahoma City and Guthrie each had populations of at least 10,000, representing one of the most dramatic episodes of westward expansion in American history.

1915
Chlorine Gas Deployed at Second Battle of Ypres

During World War I, the German army released chlorine gas as a chemical weapon at the Second Battle of Ypres in Belgium, marking the first large-scale use of poison gas in modern warfare. The attack killed and incapacitated thousands of Allied soldiers and triggered an international arms race in chemical weapons technology.

1970
First Earth Day Celebrated

Twenty million Americans participated in the inaugural Earth Day, founded by U.S. Senator Gaylord Nelson in response to a massive 1969 oil spill in Santa Barbara, California. The event is widely credited with sparking the modern environmental movement and directly led to the creation of the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency and landmark legislation including the Clean Air Act.

1977
Optical Fiber Carries First Live Telephone Traffic

For the first time in history, optical fiber was used to carry live telephone traffic in a field trial in Long Beach, California, conducted by General Telephone. This milestone demonstrated that light-based data transmission was commercially viable and laid the groundwork for the fiber-optic networks that now carry the majority of the world's internet and telecommunications traffic.

1992
Guadalajara Gas Explosions Kill Hundreds

A series of massive explosions tore through the streets of Guadalajara, Mexico, after gasoline had leaked into the city's sewer system and ignited. The disaster killed at least 206 people, injured nearly 500, and left approximately 15,000 homeless, devastating the downtown Analco district and prompting a national reckoning over industrial safety and municipal infrastructure.

2016
Paris Agreement Signed

Representatives from 174 countries and the European Union signed the Paris Agreement on climate change at the United Nations headquarters in New York, setting a landmark goal of limiting global warming to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels. The record-breaking number of signatories on a single day reflected international consensus around the urgency of addressing greenhouse gas emissions.
