Why Insurers Are Struggling to Modernise Pricing Platforms

Despite significant investment in pricing technology and AI, insurers across EMEA are finding that production changes remain minimal, with pricing updates still taking weeks due to IT dependency, fragmented governance, and AI that generates insights but cannot be operationalized into live rating engines. On This Day · April 30

1789
George Washington Inaugurated as First U.S. President

George Washington was sworn in as the first President of the United States at Federal Hall in New York City, then the nation's capital. He took the oath of office administered by Chancellor Robert Livingston before a crowd of thousands, marking the launch of the American constitutional republic. His presidency set foundational precedents for the executive branch that endure to this day.

1803
Louisiana Purchase Signed

The United States and France finalized the Louisiana Purchase agreement, in which the U.S. acquired approximately 828,000 square miles of territory west of the Mississippi River for roughly $15 million. The deal effectively doubled the size of the young nation and was negotiated by American envoys Robert Livingston and James Monroe with French Foreign Minister Talleyrand. The purchase opened vast new lands for westward expansion and remains one of the largest territorial acquisitions in history.

1900
Casey Jones Dies in Famous Train Wreck

Locomotive engineer John Luther "Casey" Jones died when his Illinois Central passenger train, the Cannonball Express, collided with a stalled freight train near Vaughan, Mississippi. Jones reportedly stayed at the controls to slow the train and warned his fireman to jump, saving the fireman's life while sacrificing his own. His heroism was immortalized in a popular ballad and became one of the most enduring legends in American folklore.

1945
Adolf Hitler Dies by Suicide in Berlin Bunker

With Soviet forces closing in on central Berlin, Adolf Hitler took his own life in his underground Führerbunker, along with his wife Eva Braun, whom he had married the previous day. His death effectively ended the Nazi regime, and Germany surrendered unconditionally to the Allied powers just eight days later on May 8, 1945. Hitler's suicide marked the collapse of the Third Reich after nearly twelve years of totalitarian rule and a war that claimed tens of millions of lives.

1975
Fall of Saigon — Vietnam War Ends

North Vietnamese forces captured Saigon, the capital of South Vietnam, completing their conquest of the country and ending the Vietnam War. The final hours saw a frantic U.S. helicopter evacuation — Operation Frequent Wind — lifting thousands of American personnel and South Vietnamese civilians from rooftops across the city. The fall marked a decisive communist victory and the reunification of Vietnam under Hanoi's government, ending two decades of direct American military involvement in Southeast Asia.

1492
Columbus Granted Charter for New World Voyage

Spanish monarchs Ferdinand II and Isabella I signed the Capitulations of Santa Fe, a contract granting Christopher Columbus the titles, powers, and privileges he requested in exchange for leading an exploratory voyage westward across the Atlantic. The agreement gave Columbus the rank of Admiral and the right to govern any lands he discovered. The voyage that followed later that year fundamentally altered the course of world history by connecting the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.

1993
World Wide Web Released to the Public Domain

CERN, the European Organization for Nuclear Research, announced that the World Wide Web technology developed by Tim Berners-Lee would be placed in the public domain, free for anyone to use without royalties or restrictions. The decision ensured the Web could grow as an open, universal platform rather than a proprietary system. This single act of openness catalyzed the explosive growth of the internet and reshaped global communication, commerce, and culture.

1812
Louisiana Becomes the 18th U.S. State

Louisiana was admitted to the Union as the 18th state, the first state to be carved from the territory acquired in the Louisiana Purchase of 1803. It was also the first state in the union with a majority non-Anglo-Saxon population, reflecting its rich French, Spanish, and African cultural heritage. Louisiana's admission expanded the nation's southern frontier and brought the strategically vital port of New Orleans firmly under U.S. sovereignty.

1961
First American Travels to Space (Mercury-Redstone 3)

Alan Shepard became the first American in space aboard Freedom 7, completing a 15-minute suborbital flight that reached an altitude of 116 miles before splashing down in the Atlantic Ocean. The flight came just 23 days after Soviet cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin's orbital mission and demonstrated American capability in the Space Race. Shepard's flight galvanized U.S. resolve in space exploration and helped set the stage for President Kennedy's announcement of the Moon landing goal just weeks later.

1006
Supernova SN 1006 Observed — Brightest Stellar Event in Recorded History

Astronomers and observers across Europe, the Arab world, China, and Egypt recorded the appearance of a brilliant new star in the constellation Lupus — the supernova now catalogued as SN 1006. At its peak it was reportedly bright enough to cast shadows at night and visible in daylight, making it the brightest stellar event ever reliably documented by humans. The remnant of this stellar explosion, located about 7,200 light-years away, is still studied by modern astronomers as one of the best-preserved supernova remnants in the galaxy.
