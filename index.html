On This Day · April 21

753 BC
Founding of Rome

According to Roman tradition, Romulus founded the city of Rome on this date, establishing what would become the capital of one of history's greatest empires. The date was calculated by Roman scholars and remained the cornerstone of the Roman calendar system.

1509
Henry VIII Ascends the English Throne

Henry VIII became King of England following the death of his father, Henry VII, beginning one of the most transformative reigns in English history. He would later break with the Catholic Church to establish the Church of England, reshaping the religious and political landscape of the nation.

1526
First Battle of Panipat — Mughal Empire Founded

Babur, ruler of Kabul, defeated and killed Ibrahim Lodi, the last sultan of the Delhi Sultanate, at the First Battle of Panipat in northern India. This victory established the Mughal Empire, which would rule the Indian subcontinent for more than three centuries.

1782
Bangkok Founded as Thai Capital

King Buddha Yodfa Chulaloke (Rama I) founded the city of Rattanakosin on the eastern bank of the Chao Phraya River, establishing what is now known internationally as Bangkok. The city became the capital of the Kingdom of Siam and remains Thailand's capital and largest city today.

1836
Battle of San Jacinto — Texas Independence

Texan forces under General Sam Houston decisively defeated the Mexican army of General Antonio López de Santa Anna in an 18-minute battle near present-day Houston, Texas. The victory secured Texan independence from Mexico and led to the establishment of the Republic of Texas.

1898
Spanish-American War Begins

The United States Navy began a blockade of Cuban ports, marking the effective start of the Spanish-American War, which Congress formally declared on April 25. The brief conflict ended Spanish colonial rule in Cuba, Puerto Rico, and the Philippines, establishing the U.S. as a major world power.

1918
The Red Baron Shot Down

German World War I flying ace Manfred von Richthofen, known as "The Red Baron," was shot down and killed over Vaux-sur-Somme in France. Credited with 80 aerial victories — the most of any pilot in the war — he was buried with full military honors by Allied forces.

1960
Brasília Inaugurated as Brazil's Capital

Brazil officially inaugurated Brasília as its new capital, replacing Rio de Janeiro, in a ceremony transferring the three branches of government simultaneously. The modernist city, designed by urban planner Lúcio Costa and architect Oscar Niemeyer, was built from scratch in just 41 months in the country's interior.

1972
Apollo 16 Lands on the Moon

Astronauts John Young and Charles Duke piloted the Apollo 16 lunar module to the surface of the Moon in the Descartes Highlands, marking the fifth crewed lunar landing in NASA history. During their three-day stay, they collected 95.8 kg of lunar samples and deployed scientific instruments to study the Moon's geology.

1992
First Confirmed Exoplanets Discovered

Polish astronomer Aleksander Wolszczan announced the discovery of two planets orbiting the pulsar PSR 1257+12, confirmed as the first exoplanets ever detected beyond our solar system. The discovery fundamentally changed astronomy's understanding of planetary formation and opened the field of exoplanet science.
