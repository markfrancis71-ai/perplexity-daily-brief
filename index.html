On This Day · May 1

1707
Acts of Union: Great Britain Formed

The Acts of Union came into effect on May 1, 1707, uniting the Kingdom of England and the Kingdom of Scotland into a single sovereign state: the Kingdom of Great Britain. The union merged the two parliaments into one at Westminster, reshaping the political map of the British Isles. It laid the constitutional foundation for what would eventually become the United Kingdom.

1840
World's First Adhesive Postage Stamp Issued

The Penny Black, the world's first adhesive postage stamp used in a public postal system, went on sale in the United Kingdom on May 1, 1840. Featuring a profile of Queen Victoria, it standardized postal rates and made written communication accessible to ordinary people regardless of distance. The innovation revolutionized global communications and spurred similar stamp programs across the world.

1886
Haymarket Affair and the Birth of International Workers' Day

On May 1, 1886, more than 300,000 workers across the United States launched a general strike demanding an eight-hour workday, with the largest demonstrations centered in Chicago. The movement culminated in the Haymarket affair on May 4, forever linking May 1 to labor rights. In 1889, the International Socialist Congress designated May 1 as International Workers' Day — now observed in over 160 countries.

1915
RMS Lusitania Departs on Final Voyage

The British ocean liner RMS Lusitania departed New York on May 1, 1915, for what would be her last transatlantic crossing. Six days later, on May 7, she was torpedoed by a German U-boat off the coast of Ireland, sinking in just 18 minutes and killing 1,198 of the 1,962 people on board. The disaster inflamed public opinion against Germany and is widely considered a pivotal event in drawing the United States toward entering World War I.

1931
Empire State Building Dedicated

President Herbert Hoover officially dedicated the Empire State Building in New York City on May 1, 1931, by pressing a button in Washington D.C. to turn on the skyscraper's lights. Completed in just 410 days, the 102-story tower stood as the world's tallest building for nearly 40 years. It became one of the most iconic symbols of American ambition and 20th-century engineering.

1960
U-2 Spy Plane Incident

On May 1, 1960, an American U-2 reconnaissance aircraft piloted by Francis Gary Powers was shot down over Soviet airspace, causing a major Cold War crisis. The Eisenhower administration initially denied the nature of the flight, but was forced to admit to the espionage mission when the Soviets produced both the wreckage and the captured pilot. The incident derailed a planned superpower summit in Paris and severely worsened U.S.–Soviet relations.

1994
Ayrton Senna Killed at San Marino Grand Prix

Three-time Formula One World Champion Ayrton Senna died on May 1, 1994, after his Williams car crashed into a concrete barrier during the San Marino Grand Prix at Imola, Italy. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest racing drivers of all time, and his death prompted sweeping safety reforms throughout motorsport. Senna's passing shocked the sporting world and remains one of the most tragic days in Formula One history.

2003
Bush Declares "Mission Accomplished" in Iraq

On May 1, 2003, President George W. Bush landed on the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln and delivered a speech beneath a banner reading "Mission Accomplished," declaring the end of major combat operations in Iraq. The speech was intended to mark the fall of Saddam Hussein's government, but the subsequent years of insurgency made the declaration deeply controversial. It became one of the most debated political moments of the Iraq War era.

2011
Osama bin Laden Killed in U.S. Raid

In the early hours of May 2 Pakistan time (May 1 in Washington D.C.), U.S. Navy SEALs from SEAL Team Six killed al-Qaeda founder Osama bin Laden during a covert raid on his compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan. President Barack Obama announced the operation to the nation late on the night of May 1, 2011, nearly ten years after the September 11 attacks. The mission, codenamed Operation Neptune Spear, marked a defining moment in the U.S.-led War on Terror.

2019
Emperor Naruhito Ascends Japan's Chrysanthemum Throne

On May 1, 2019, Naruhito formally ascended to Japan's Chrysanthemum Throne, beginning the Reiwa imperial era after his father Akihito's historic abdication — the first such abdication in over 200 years. The transition was a moment of national celebration in Japan, with elaborate ceremonies marking the start of the new era whose name means "beautiful harmony." Naruhito became the 126th emperor in the world's oldest continuous hereditary monarchy.
